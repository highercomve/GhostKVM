Software KVM for Linux and any Bluetooth device

One keyboard and mouse. Two computers.

GhostKVM turns your Linux desktop into a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse. Push the pointer off the edge of your screen and keep working on your Chromebook, tablet or phone, as if it were an extra display.

How it works

Your desktop becomes a wireless keyboard and mouse.

ChromeOS doesn't let apps capture or create input for the whole system, so other software KVMs can't run there. GhostKVM needs nothing on the Chromebook: it pairs with your desktop like with any Bluetooth keyboard.

  1. 1

    Pair once

    On the Chromebook, open Settings › Bluetooth and pair GhostKVM. From then on it reconnects by itself.

  2. 2

    Place it

    Drag the Chromebook to the side of your monitor where it sits on your desk: left, right, above or below.

  3. 3

    Push past the edge

    Move the pointer off that edge. Your keyboard and mouse now drive the Chromebook. Come back the same way, or press Ctrl+Alt+K.

Features

Feels like one big desk.

An extra display, not a toggle

The pointer leaves your monitor and appears on the Chromebook at the same height, then comes back where you left. No key to remember.

Nothing on the Chromebook

It's a standard Bluetooth LE keyboard and mouse. No extension, no Linux container, no developer mode.

Keeps your Bluetooth mouse

One adapter talks to your Bluetooth mouse and to the Chromebook at the same time. Tested with an Intel AX210.

Never leaves a key stuck

It won't switch mid-drag or while a key is held. If the Chromebook disconnects or GhostKVM stops, your devices go straight back to the desktop.

Lives in your tray

Close the window and GhostKVM keeps running. The ghost shows the link state at a glance.

Updates itself, safely

GhostKVM checks for a release once a day and installs it only when its signature matches the key built into the app. Packages update through your package manager.

Scriptable

ghostkvm ctl types, clicks and presses shortcuts on the Chromebook from scripts, automation tools and AI agents, while you keep working on the desktop.

Works on your desktop

Edge switching on Hyprland, every X11 desktop, and GNOME 45+ and KDE Plasma 6.1+ on Wayland through the InputCapture portal. Elsewhere, switch with the hotkey or the tray.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

Download the latest release: a tarball that updates itself, or a .deb, .rpm or Arch package. Or build it from source.

  • Desktop any Linux desktop; edge switching on Hyprland, X11, GNOME and KDE
  • Bluetooth BlueZ 5 and an adapter with LE peripheral support
  • Permissions your user in the input group, no root
  • Other computer a Chromebook, or anything that takes a BLE keyboard and mouse
Install the tarball (updates itself)
curl -fsSLO https://github.com/highercomve/GhostKVM/releases/latest/download/ghostkvm-linux-$(uname -m).tar.xz
tar -xJf ghostkvm-linux-$(uname -m).tar.xz
install -Dm755 ghostkvm-linux-$(uname -m)/bin/ghostkvm ~/.local/bin/ghostkvm
Or build from source
git clone https://github.com/highercomve/GhostKVM.git
cd GhostKVM && make install-local
Allow reading keyboards and mice
sudo usermod -aG input "$USER"   # then log out and back in
Drive the Chromebook from a script
ghostkvm ctl key search
ghostkvm ctl type "chrome://settings"
ghostkvm ctl key Return

Help

Good to know

Does it only work with Chromebooks?

No. The Chromebook is the reason GhostKVM exists (it can't run a helper app), but your desktop shows up as a standard Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, so anything that accepts one works: Android tablets and phones, iPads, Macs, Windows PCs and TVs. GhostKVM shows the device by its own Bluetooth name.

Does it work on GNOME or KDE?

Yes. On GNOME 45+ and KDE Plasma 6.1+ under Wayland, GhostKVM asks the desktop through the InputCapture portal to tell it when the pointer crosses the edge; the desktop asks you to allow that the first time. X11 sessions and Hyprland work directly. On other Wayland desktops, such as Sway, switch with Ctrl+Alt+K or the tray, and come back by pushing past the Chromebook's edge.

The Chromebook doesn't list GhostKVM

Your desktop must not be discoverable as a normal Bluetooth computer at the same time, or the Chromebook merges the two and shows only the computer's name. GhostKVM turns that off while it runs. If another app made the desktop discoverable, turn it off there, then switch the Chromebook's Bluetooth off and on.

The pointer lands at the wrong height

ChromeOS never reports where its pointer is, so GhostKVM tracks it. Make sure the resolution in GhostKVM matches the Chromebook's (Settings › Device › Displays), and turn off Mouse acceleration on the Chromebook. Use GhostKVM's Pointer speed instead.

The pointer lags, then jumps

Bluetooth LE carries a limited number of updates per second. GhostKVM sends one combined update every 15 ms to stay within it. If it still lags, move the Chromebook closer or reduce other Bluetooth traffic on the desktop.

Something feels stuck

Press Ctrl+Alt+K: it works from either computer. Quitting GhostKVM from the tray always hands your keyboard and mouse back to the desktop.
